# Forced-bag proof binding

Proof creation already appended the uniquely deducible final public bag member.
Ordinary retention instead built its resource binding and replayed stored result
contexts from the shorter raw queue. The same public position could therefore
produce a valid certificate that its consumer immediately rejected.

`st-obligation-projection.js` and `st-obligation-route.js` supply the current
finite-path normalization used by creation, ordinary revalidation, action
advancement and retained post-lock suffix replay. Literal resource binding
remains separate from that hypothetical supply. A route appends only a unique
public remainder, consumes the deduction once, and does not append it again. Missing, invalid, contradictory or ambiguous bag knowledge
adds nothing. No hidden future, private generator, original-bag age or observed
piece identity is inferred. A later actual reveal obtains its real public inventory identity.

This does not relax certificate checks: raw observation context, current obligation
Context, exact supplied resource binding, per-step result contexts, legal actions,
resource obligations, notches and final access remain enforced. Tests cover the
original producer/consumer mismatch, HOLD and Undo, actual reveals, identical
letters with distinct identities, a genuine retained scoring suffix, unknown
bags and forged state/history/bindings. Two old scheduling roots now require
exact retention rather than documenting the former false rejection.

Native schema-v2 sessions preserve current inventory and obligations. Obsolete
history-tape sessions are rejected; no silent position-only downgrade occurs.

No handoff budget, node/time limit, search scheduling, ranking or geometry policy
is changed here. A separate larger-handoff experiment passed finite-proof tests
but regressed matched play; it is deliberately excluded. Correct local proof
retention is not by itself evidence of improved long-run ST play.
