# Reset coach

Reset is separate from the ordinary early/late Midgame scorers and the old finite
paired-T research certificate. Its runtime contract is two actual T placements
wholly in Left (columns 1–4), each clearing zero lines. The second such lock ends
Reset immediately. Midgame owns any rebuilding afterward; a third visible T,
subsequent TSD, parity pattern or balanced endpoint is not a completion condition.

## Entry and information

`st-reset-policy.js` considers Reset only with replay-verified empty-origin history,
a qualified opening allocation, and exhausted ordinary extra-Z balance. It checks
Left and Center independently against the two rows required for the next TSD.
Center column 7 is reserved for the T in the upper row but is support in the lower
row. The active piece, HOLD, six-piece preview and public forced-bag deductions
bound the earliest T arrival. The four-cells-per-intervening-piece capacity is an
optimistic necessary bound, not a claim those pieces can actually rebuild support.
A positive result is consideration, not proof that ordinary play is impossible.

`st-reset-coach.js` owns bounded actual-pose search and validation. Every suggested
prefix retains normal clear, notch, headroom, newly sealed-cell, covered-cell
repair/access, Right allocation and exact piece accounting checks. Only the two
qualifying T placements receive the scoped non-scoring-T exception. Filler I
quads are not reclassified as Reset completion. The coach can execute safe visible
prefixes while waiting for another T across new previews and bags; it never
promises an unseen piece or substitutes a seed for public evidence.

## Persistent accounting

`st-resource-history.js` owns immutable selection markers, actual qualifying T
identities, progress and completed episodes. Markers cross worker/session boundaries
only through the existing public action/reveal replay, and their eligibility is
rechecked at the actual selection state. HOLD changes possession, not identity or
bag membership. Undo restores the earlier immutable ledger. Imported positions
without complete empty-origin history remain out of scope; serialized status,
credit or phase flags have no authority.

The verified opening has two nonscoring Left Ts and one mandatory non-Right Z.
Consequently the ordinary starting surplus is one. The conserved balance is:

- capacity = 1 + actual qualifying Reset T identities;
- remaining = capacity − all subsequent actual non-Right Z allocations.

The already-spent optional Z remains subtracted, including spending before Reset
entry or between its Ts. A second T is not a fixed allowance grant. Zero, one or
two additional Z allocations can leave different balances. The equivalent count
identity TL − ZN = (ZR − TR) + (LT − LZ) follows by classifying all actual T/Z
locks: Left T, non-Right Z, Right Z and Right TSD. Current cap inventory must not
be subtracted a second time. Whether a particular visible Z is available for
non-Right work still depends independently on executable Right cap/cleanup duties.

No S/L obligation, pending Center completion or piece/bag identity is erased at
Reset entry or completion. Active Reset exposes its conserved balance to its own
coach, while ordinary early/late dispatch resumes only after the second T.

## Evidence boundaries

diagnostics/controls, not seven certified Reset boundaries. Their synthetic

The count invariant above is a conservation deduction, not a newly attributed
community formula. Finite verified prefixes do not establish indefinite survival.

Generated protocols, smoke runs, source snapshots, full-suite manifests and
canonical reports are retained outside Git. Correctness, aggregate regression
and exploration are separate. Total game lines and post-first-Reset lines are
reported separately; the general 85-line post-Reset goal is not an entry rule or
implicit acceptance gate.

## Pre-entry ordinary proof preference

A support-risk trigger is consideration, not an instruction to discard an
executable ordinary scoring proof. Before selecting a new Reset, the dispatcher
revalidates an existing ordinary commitment/previous advice. It prefers that
exact route only if it contains a visible TSD or its non-T prefix resolves both
scoring-row support deficits. A fresh ordinary search is preferred only when its
fully checked public continuation contains an actual TSD and no diverted T.
An active Reset never takes this branch: its two-T completion contract remains.

The pre-entry comparison shares the caller's deadline and node budget. Ordinary
work, including retained-proof validation, receives at most 80% of nodes and two
thirds of remaining wall time; 256 nodes within that ordinary allocation are
reserved for literal preference verification. All spent work is reported even
when the ordinary candidate is not preferred. Reset receives only the remaining
budget. Disabled HOLD is enforced again at the preference replay boundary.

Reset's expansion beam is still bounded by its configured width. Certification
scans already-enumerated ranked candidates within the same budget rather than
allowing failed top-ranked certificates to hide a lower-ranked valid prefix.
The three public-history regression fixtures document actual development misses;
they are not special-case seed or board inputs to production logic.
